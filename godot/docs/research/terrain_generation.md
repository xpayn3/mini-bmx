# Upgrading a noise-sum world without losing statelessness

**Verdict: don't simulate erosion; make the height field smarter in place, and use the erosion look only as a gated, last-step filter.** Grid and graph methods (stream-power erosion, tile erosion, Génevaux/Cordonnier river-first terrain, GANs) need drainage area or a global solve, so they break the per-point, infinite, streamed contract and are not worth pursuing for 48 m chunks. The techniques that fit are all pure functions of (x, z, seed): (1) slope/height/curvature-aware biome and material masks, which can be baked per chunk without touching the height function; (2) a one-level domain warp of the land octaves; (3) Minecraft-1.18-style low-frequency "control" noises remapped through splines to steer amplitude and offset; (4) Quilez-style slope-damped fBm on analytic-gradient noise with integer hashes; (5) talus and valley-flat shaping; (6) strata/terraces in the existing `b_rug` canyon biome; and, as the large optional item, (7) the Johansen/Phacelle erosion filter, which is stateless but 10-30x the cost of an octave (inference) and must be gated away from the trail and likely baked into chunk grids. A stateless river network is possible only as your own composition (jittered hash cells plus distance-to-polyline carve); no source supplies it, so it ranks last. The exactness constraint is manageable: use integer hashes and polynomial replacements for `exp`/`sin`/`cos`, and the C# mirror stops being the problem. The licence constraint is real: the erosion-filter code is MPL-2.0, and an older variant it descends from carries CC BY-NC-SA, which a Steam release cannot use.

## What your field has and what the sources say it lacks

Your land is `landform`: gradient-noise octaves at 28/90/240 m, a 260 m `relief` that modulates amplitude, two 700 m masks (`big`, `rdg`) that switch on the 240 m octave and a ridged term, plus the `hill()` mountain ranges (`range_at`), a `wild()` term, and trail blending in `base()`. The biome masks `b_dry` and `b_rug` already have a 160 m domain-warp hook (60 m amplitude), but the land itself has no warp, no erosion, no analytic derivatives (gradients are finite differences, e.g. `pathN` at 0.5 m and the berm Hessian at 2 m), and no river network. Biome is a function of (x, z) only, blind to height and slope. Your repo notes already record "hydrology-lite gullies" as a deferred size-L item (item #17 in `papers_review_2026-11-23.md`, citing Génevaux 2013 and Galin 2010), and "flow ground" (#2) was measured and dropped because only ~1 % of open land throws the bike at 30 km/h. So the case for any height upgrade is visual, not ride-feel, with the exception of valley flats and talus, which affect rideability.

Industry practice supports the same split. Studios separate macro shape, detail and placement, and the heightfield itself is usually authored or baked offline. Unreal's PCG framework decorates a landscape per grid cell with a time budget and a hierarchy of cell sizes; it does not generate heights ([Epic: PCG generation modes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-pcg-generation-modes-in-unreal-engine)). Guerrilla's Horizon pipeline turns density maps into deterministic, locally stable objects by ordered dithering, with 500+ asset types and ~250 µs average GPU time, fed by painted world-data seeded from World Machine/Houdini ([Van Muijden, GDC 2017](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/GDC2017_VanMuijden_GPUBasedProceduralPlacementInHorizonZeroDawn.pdf)). Far Cry 5 used Houdini for biomes, freshwater networks and cliff rocks, but Ubisoft says the map as a whole is handcrafted ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2018/03/22/the-procedural-world-generation-of-far-cry-5/)). Houdini's erosion node is valuable mostly for its by-products (flow, debris, sediment layers) used as texture and scatter masks ([SideFX](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/heightfields/erosion.html)). The closest design analogue to your game is Riders Republic: path first, terrain sculpted to the path, with candidate trails scored on slope and curvature ([Ubisoft News](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/7Jdttfpxq3rykQWpGsVFDa)); your trail-first blend in `base()` already matches that order. The only truly stateless "ecotope" idea worth taking from these is the pure density function `density(position, fields)`, which is what your chunk builders already are.

## Stateless versus grid-bound: the compatibility table

| Technique | Per-point stateless? | Needs | Verdict for this game |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slope/height/curvature masks (biome, material, scatter) | Yes (derived from the height function) | Gradient (finite difference is fine) | Fits; bake in chunk worker |
| Domain warp of land octaves | Yes | 1-2 extra low-octave noise evals | Fits |
| Minecraft-style spline controls ([Minecraft Wiki](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)) | Yes | 2-3 very low-frequency noises + piecewise splines | Fits |
| Slope-damped fBm (Quilez "morenoise") | Yes | Analytic-gradient noise | Fits; changes every height |
| Ridged/hybrid multifractal | Yes | Value noise only | Fits; formulas unverified |
| Talus clamp, valley flats, strata/terraces | Yes | Gradient + masks | Fits |
| Johansen/Phacelle erosion filter ([blog](https://blog.runevision.com/2026/03/)) | Yes, "every point evaluated in isolation" | Height + gradient, 4x4 cells per octave | Fits if gated or baked |
| Jittered-cell river network + valley carve | Yes, bounded 3x3-cell neighbourhood (inference) | Your own design | Fits; large effort, unproven |
| Dendry dendritic distance function ([Gaillard 2019](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/cgvlab/www/resources/papers/Gaillard-2019-Dendry_A_Procedural_Model_for_Dendritic_Patterns.pdf)) | Described as built on the fly and evaluable in parallel | Paper-level only (not read in full) | Candidate for rivers; unverified |
| Génevaux 2013 river-first terrain ([HAL](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01339224)) | Needs a river graph | Graph pre-grown on a worker (like `grow_course`) | Possible, large |
| Stream-power erosion (Cordonnier 2016, Schott 2023) | No | Global/regional drainage area | Skip |
| Tile/grid erosion ([arXiv 2210.14496](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.14496)) | No | 100 iterations, 256² takes 7.5 s; no seam handling in the paper | Skip |
| Hnaidi 2010 curve constraints, Guérin 2017 GANs | No | Global multigrid solve / trained networks | Skip |

The decisive reason to skip the simulation family is boundary conditions: drainage depends on the whole upstream basin, and a tile with a margin long enough to capture flow paths would cost a multiple of the tile itself (inference; the tile-erosion paper reports no seam or overlap analysis).

## Ranked upgrades, with effort, risk and verification

Effort is S (hours), M (a day or two), L (a week or more). All "visual change" notes are inference from the techniques' descriptions, because none of these has been prototyped on your field. Anything that changes the height function re-lays every world's trail past the first section (the trail follows the smoothed land) and moves lakes; you have accepted that before, but note it.

**1. Slope/height/curvature-aware biome and material masks (S, low risk).** Make biome depend on elevation (a temperature proxy), moisture and slope, which is how Red Blob's noise-based maps work, with elevation and moisture as independent maps in a Whittaker-style lookup ([Red Blob](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/terrain-from-noise/)), and add scree/rock on steep faces and an alpine band by height; Unreal PCG filters on steepness and altitude the same way ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/3d/make-a-natural-environment-with-procedural-tools-in-unreal-engine-5)). Visually: the same land reads as far more natural, with no new geometry. Your chunk worker already bakes `grass_img` and `dirt_tex` per texel, so slope and curvature masks can be computed there from the existing height (curvature-driven rules are inference only; no source was found). Verify: `terrain_cs_cmp` stays EXACT if the height function is untouched; if `biome()` changes, it must be mirrored in `TerrainFieldCs`, so run it anyway, plus `deco_probe` for tree density.

**2. One-level domain warp of the land octaves (S, low-medium risk).** Today only the biome masks are warped. Warping the 28/90/240 m octaves' sample positions by a low-frequency noise breaks the lattice alignment and meanders ridges; the Quilez form `f(p + k·fbm(p))` with k around 0.1-0.3 of the feature wavelength is recalled, not verified in-session. Cost is one or two extra low-octave evaluations per point. Verify: `terrain_cs_cmp`, `trail_grow_probe`, `rope_find` (canyons depend on the field), and `route_count` to see route-feature counts shift.

**3. Spline-remapped low-frequency controls (S-M, low risk).** Minecraft 1.18 derives shape from continentalness, erosion and peaks-and-valleys noises passed through splines to an offset, a vertical factor and jaggedness ([Minecraft Wiki](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)); the wiki quotes erosion band boundaries (-1.0, -0.78, -0.375, -0.2225, 0.05, 0.45, 0.55, 1.0), but the spline control points themselves were not retrieved, so the shape of the curves is yours to design. For you: 2-3 noises with 2-8 km periods modulate the existing amplitudes, base offset and the strength of `rdg`/`big`, replacing the current ad-hoc 700 m masks with a controllable macro layer, while the trail keeps its own profile. Splines are plain float math (cubic Hermite with matched tangents gives smooth slope), so they are trivially bit-exact. Verify: as #2, plus `offtrail_probe`.

**4. Slope-damped fBm on analytic-gradient noise (M, medium risk).** Quilez's trick sums each octave's analytic gradient and divides each later octave's amplitude by `1 + |Σ∇|²`, so steep ground stays smooth and flats keep detail; quoted code: `dsum += d; sum += amp*n/(1+|dsum|²)` ([Decarpentier](https://www.decarpentier.nl/scape-procedural-basics)). It costs roughly 1.3x a plain octave sum (estimate), and gives you analytic gradients to replace the finite differences in `pathN` and the slope logic. Caveats: the gradient of the damped sum is only approximate in practice (exact needs second derivatives), and Quilez notes discontinuity artefacts from non-continuous second derivatives of the base noise ([morenoise](https://iquilezles.org/articles/morenoise), snippet-level only). Visually: "bumpy flats, smooth steeps". It changes every height, so it is the most invasive of the cheap items; apply it to the 28 m and 90 m detail octaves, gated by the existing masks. Ridged/billow terms need the chain rule through `abs` (sign-flipped gradient), and creases give gradient discontinuities that you will feel as a ridge line.

**5. Talus clamp and valley flats (S-M, medium risk for ride feel).** Soft-limit the gradient to a talus slope, and blend noise amplitude toward a flat floor by a valley mask. These are my design suggestions with no source tested, but they are the only upgrades in this list that improve rideability, and they dovetail with your measured braking-energy finding (the trail's bends do not fit its descents). Keep eroded detail amplitude below the trail-smoothing scale or the trail will not feel it. Verify: `bend_probe`, `berm_probe`, the demo-AI 130 s runs, the slope suite.

**6. Strata, terraces and mesas in the `b_rug` canyon biome (M, low risk).** Houdini's Terrace outputs separate cliff and mesa-top masks and Strata adds noise-based rock bands ([SideFX terracing](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/heightfields_cop/terracing.html), [strata](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/heightfields_cop/strata.html)); Red Blob quantises elevation for terraces ([Red Blob](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/terrain-from-noise/)). A smooth stepped remap (not `floor`) gated by `b_rug` and slope gives caprock and bands, and yields cliff/top masks for the ground shader. It overlaps nothing you have except the rope-bridge canyon faces. Verify: `terrain_cs_cmp`, `rope_find`.

**7. Erosion filter on mountain flanks (L, medium-high risk).** Johansen's filter adds branching gullies and sharp ridges to any height function that returns height and gradient; per octave it runs stripe waves aligned to the gradient over a grid of cells with random pivots, blends neighbours, and lets large gullies suppress small ones; 5 octaves by default ([runevision blog](https://blog.runevision.com/2026/03/)). It is explicitly stateless and chunk-friendly, but has known limits: derivatives are only approximate, the altitude-based fade target is not robust, drainage lines can stop mid-slope, and the look is tuned for mountain-scale slopes ([blog](https://blog.runevision.com/2026/03/); [bevy_erosion_filter README](https://docs.rs/crate/bevy_erosion_filter/latest/source/README.md)). It needs height plus gradient, so it pairs naturally with #4. Cost is the issue: 5 octaves × 16 cells is 80 cell evaluations per sample against your 1.4-3.8 µs height (the 10-30x figure is my estimate; no timings exist in any source). Mitigations (inference): 2-3 octaves for physics reads; gate by distance to the trail and by slope so rideable ground is untouched; apply it to the pre-trail `wild` field and let the trail blend win; or bake it into the per-chunk height grids on workers. Pivots must come from your integer hashes. Visually: the single largest step toward a "real mountain" look. Verify: `terrain_cs_cmp` on the polynomialised version, `trail_grow_probe`, `wood_edge_probe` (`walls=0`), a frame-profiler check for height cost.

**8. Stateless river network (L, high risk, unproven).** The composition proposed in the notes: jittered hash cells, each cell's seed routes to a deterministic lower neighbour using the smooth large-scale height, polyline segments between seeds with hashed jitter, then valley carve by distance to the nearest segments over a 3×3 cell neighbourhood, plus a riparian moisture mask. No source provides this; caveats are that rivers will not truly merge by flow accumulation and need continuity with `WATER`. The nearest published analogues are Dendry (distance to an implicit dendritic tree, built on the fly, per the abstract-level description) and recursive-subdivision rivers (Mogensen and Isenbecker, [arXiv 2504.08383](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08383v1)), which report grid-aligned artefacts and rivers that don't always follow steepest descent. Génevaux-style river-first generation is more geomorphically correct ([Génevaux PDF](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Genevaux13ToG.pdf)) but needs a graph grown on a worker, like your `grow_course`. Verify: a new probe analogous to `trail_grow_probe`; `terrain_cs_cmp`; check lakes and canyons do not collide.

**9. Density-map scatter for rocks and trees (M, separate from height).** Replace the rejected trunk-gap rule with Guerrilla-style density × deterministic dithering, with the trail's inverse mask as a clearing; this is placement, not terrain, and mostly independent of the above (see the 2026-11-23 findings that gap rules alone did not improve free riding).

## Making C# match GDScript exactly

Your constraint is stricter than most: the C# port reproduces GDScript's `sin`/`exp`/`log` bit for bit and falls back to an engine `Expression` on near-ties (which also caused the map-sampling quit crash until `_approx` was added). Any technique that calls transcendentals per sample inherits that cost. The erosion filter and Phacelle use `exp`, `cos` and `sin` heavily (kernel weight `exp(-2d²)`, phase `cos`/`sin`). The mitigation recommended in the notes (design advice, not sourced): replace them with polynomial or minimax approximations and bump kernels such as `max(1 - d²/r², 0)²`, which are pure add/multiply and faster as well; use integer hashes on int32 lattice coordinates plus the seed, as your `range_at` already does, instead of Quilez's float `fract`-based hashes (not bit-exact across CPU/GPU); keep all arithmetic in one precision with explicit evaluation order, because C# may fuse multiply-adds differently than GDScript's doubles. Splines, domain warp, slope damping and terraces need only add/multiply/smoothstep and are exact by construction once the base noise is. Whichever you adopt, the one acceptance test is the existing `terrain_cs_cmp` reporting EXACT (≈736k comparisons, 0 mismatches), followed by `trail_grow_probe` and `rope_find` PASS and `wood_edge_probe` `walls=0`.

Seams are a non-issue for pointwise techniques provided the fade target or any normalisation is a global function of base height rather than per-chunk statistics ([runevision blog](https://blog.runevision.com/2026/03/); seam reasoning is inference).

## What we could not verify

The researchers state these gaps, and several claims in this report are inference:

- **Primary sources unread.** Génevaux 2013 and Cordonnier 2016 full texts were not read (abstracts and landing pages only); Quilez's articles all returned errors, so his formulas come from secondary quotes and recall; the Runevision blog gave prose and parameters but no verbatim code, so details rely on the bevy crate's Rust port summary. The 2026 deterministic-rivers write-up ("plate-local river generation") could not be fetched, and Gain et al. 2015 "Terrain sketching", Cordonnier 2017/2018 detail, and GPU erosion papers were not found.
- **No performance numbers.** No source gives ms per sample or per chunk for the erosion filter, Dendry, or tile erosion with overlap. The "10-30x one octave" and "~1.3x for damped fBm" figures are my estimates, not measurements; profile before committing.
- **Inference rather than sourced:** the stateless river network design; talus/valley-flat formulas; curvature-driven material rules; the cost of margin-based tile erosion; the claim that the polynomial replacements preserve the look; the recalled forms of domain warp, Musgrave ridged/hybrid multifractals and Swiss/Jordan noise; and the statements about how gradient approximations will feel while riding.
- **Industry gaps.** GDC Vault talks are paywalled, so Far Cry 5, No Man's Sky and Ghost of Tsushima details are secondary; No Man's Sky's domain-warping claim is from a blog only; Valheim's rules come from community reverse-engineering; Minecraft's spline control points were not retrieved. No source was found on terrain flattening in Riders Republic, nor for Descenders, RDR2, Death Stranding, Flight Simulator, Tiny Glade or MX vs ATV terrain pipelines. A "Samurai Landscapes" Tsushima talk could not be confirmed.
- **Audit limits.** The audit of `terrain_field.gd` covered lines 443-758 only (not `wild`, `noise`, `query` or the trail course); `papers_review_2026-11-23.md` was grep-read, so other terrain items there may exist; the `GameResearch/notes/` folder the README mentions is absent.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which erosion algorithm?" to "which of your masks should know about slope?". Every real simulation method fails your stateless contract, and the stateless erosion filter is the one genuinely new capability, but it is also the one with the largest cost, licence and exactness burden; its look can be approximated sooner and cheaper by items 1-6, which share one enabling step: moving the noise to integer-hashed, analytic-gradient form so slope and curvature come for free.

A sensible order is masks (no height change), then warp plus spline controls (one re-laid-world cost, paid once), then damped fBm with analytic gradients as the foundation, and only then decide whether the erosion filter is worth its gating and baking complexity after profiling a 2-3 octave prototype on a single mountain.
